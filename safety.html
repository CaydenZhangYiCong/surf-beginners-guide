Surf safety

Safety comes before style

Every surfer should respect the ocean, read conditions, and learn the basics of surf etiquette before getting in the water.

1. Learn the surf spot

Check where the rip currents, sandbars, and rocks are. If you are unsure of the break, start with a calmer, beginner-friendly beach instead of jumping into a crowded or powerful lineup.

Ask yourself

  • Is the swell small and mellow?
  • Are there rocks or strong rip currents?
  • Is the beach crowded with experienced surfers?

2. Understand rip currents

Rip currents move water away from shore. If caught in one, stay calm, don’t panic, and swim parallel to the beach until you are out of the current. Floating and conserving energy is better than fighting the water.

Remember

Panicking makes the situation worse. Float, breathe, and move with the current.

3. Respect surf etiquette

Surfing has a social code: don’t snake other surfers, wait your turn, and be aware of people already riding the wave. Respecting the lineup keeps the water safer and more enjoyable for everyone.

Basic etiquette

  • Take turns
  • Don’t paddle into another surfer’s wave
  • Be considerate of beginners

Safety checklist

Before every session

Check swell and wind conditions
Watch the shore and current patterns
Surf in a beginner-friendly lineup
Use a leash and inspect your board
Never surf alone when you are learning
Know where the nearest lifeguard or exit point is
